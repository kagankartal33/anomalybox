# Creator Brief Scorecard

Use this **creator brief scorecard** before production or approval. It is a decision tool, not a performance guarantee. Score each criterion honestly and write the missing decision beside the score.

## Scoring table

| Criterion | Score | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Audience clarity | 0–3 | Can a specific person recognise that the piece is for them? |
| Single purpose | 0–3 | Does the content have one primary job? |
| Opening alignment | 0–3 | Do the first words and first image communicate the same promise? |
| Visible proof | 0–3 | Can the central point be demonstrated or supported? |
| Production fit | 0–3 | Can the creator make it with the available time, access and confidence? |
| Distinctive angle | 0–3 | Does the piece add a point of view, example or method rather than repeat a generic list? |
| Boundary safety | 0–3 | Are claims, permissions, privacy and disclosure handled? |
| Relevant next step | 0–3 | Does the CTA match the viewer’s stage? |

### Score meanings

- **0 — Missing:** the decision has not been made.
- **1 — Vague:** the intention exists but cannot guide production.
- **2 — Usable:** the decision is clear enough to execute.
- **3 — Strong:** the decision is clear, supported and resilient to production changes.

The maximum score is 24. A low total does not mean the creator is bad at content. It shows that too many decisions are still open.

## How to interpret the result

- **0–8:** return to the audience problem and purpose before planning scenes.
- **9–15:** the idea may be useful, but the opening, evidence or production route needs work.
- **16–20:** the concept is usable; fix the lowest-scoring items before filming.
- **21–24:** the direction is well resolved. Keep the shoot focused and avoid adding unnecessary elements.

## Quick answer

In **creator brief scorecard**, connect the audience question to one opening frame and one visible form of proof. Keep the scene count within the time, access, and equipment available.

## Example

A founder plans a Reel about a product feature. Audience clarity, purpose and production fit score highly, but visible proof scores zero because the feature cannot be shown in the available prototype. The correct response is not to write a louder hook. The concept must change to a decision story, screen prototype or clearly labelled future demonstration.

## Use the scorecard in a team

Ask each reviewer to score independently before discussing. Large differences reveal hidden assumptions. Consolidate the final feedback through one owner and record the decision, not every preference.

## Common scoring mistakes

- Giving three points because the idea sounds exciting.
- Treating a trend reference as a distinctive angle.
- Counting a claim as proof.
- Ignoring privacy, disclosure or usage boundaries.
- Scoring production fit without asking the person who will make the content.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every item score three?

No. A deliberately simple piece may be publishable with several twos. Zero scores in audience, purpose, proof or safety are more important than a perfect total.

### Can this predict performance?

No. It measures readiness and clarity, not algorithmic distribution or audience response.

### Can brands use it for creator approvals?

Yes, provided it supports rather than replaces a clear brief, consolidated feedback and fair contractual terms.

## Before you use the score

Score the brief against verified product, creator and campaign details. Keep claims inside the available evidence and disclose commercial relationships. Remove any requirement that does not fit the creator’s actual access or production conditions. The score supports judgement; it does not replace an approver or guarantee performance.
