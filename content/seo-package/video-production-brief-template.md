# Video Production Brief Template for Short-Form Assets

This **video production brief template** organises video after the concept is approved. Its job is to protect must-have evidence, schedule, continuity, files and delivery.

## Quick answer

A usable **video production brief template** is a set of fields that force decisions: audience tension, opening frame, proof, spoken line, claim boundary and CTA. Copy the structure, then fill it with your product truth — empty templates do not reduce revisions by themselves.

## Copyable production brief

```text
Project: video production brief template
Concept summary:
Locations:
Talent or creator mode:
Props and products:
Shot list:
Audio and text:
Schedule:
File naming and delivery:
QA owner:
```

## Plan from must-have material outward

Mark the opening, core action, proof and ending as must-have. Add alternative angles and atmosphere only after those shots or assets are secure. A long B-roll list is not a substitute for coverage of the message.

## Schedule by setup

Group shots by location, light, product state, talent and equipment. Record the intended final scene order separately so production efficiency does not confuse the edit.

## Continuity and file control

Document wardrobe, props, product levels, screen states, lighting and hand positions where cuts need to match. Use consistent names such as `project_scene_shot_take`. Back up before changing the set.

## Audio and accessibility

Record clean dialogue, room tone and critical sound. Plan captions and meaningful on-screen text. Make sure information is not communicated only through colour or audio.

## Wrap checklist

- Must-have proof captured.
- Focus, exposure and audio checked on a real playback device.
- Releases and permissions confirmed.
- Required disclosure or labels captured.
- Files backed up in two locations.
- Missing pickups assigned before the set is cleared.

## How to fill the template without freezing

1. Write the viewer outcome in one sentence.  
2. List the proof you can show today (not ideal future proof).  
3. Script the opening 3 seconds as action + text, not a long monologue.  
4. Mark claim boundaries before you film.  
5. Record one backup ending (save CTA vs soft DM CTA).

## Common mistakes this template prevents

- Filming before the payoff is defined.  
- Using three hooks that all need different first frames.  
- Caption promising something the video never shows.  
- Leaving revision scope undefined for brand work.

Use when multiple people or multi-location shoots are involved.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a production brief needed for a phone shoot?

Yes when the content has important deliverables, claims or multiple scenes. The document can be short.

### Who owns the brief?

Name one production owner, with factual and brand approval assigned separately where needed.

### Should the brief include edit notes?

Include the intended sequence, required labels and delivery specs, but avoid dictating every cut before the footage exists.

## Crew-oriented video brief spine

- Shot list + gear minimum  
- Locations, permissions, quiet hours  
- Call sheet times and roles  
- Delivery codecs, aspect ratios, captions file  
- Backup indoor plan  

Editorial message hierarchy is assumed already approved; this brief executes the film day.

### When do I write the video brief?
After message hierarchy is approved. Film-day chaos usually means the editorial brief was skipped.

### What if I shoot alone?
Still use a short video brief: shot priority, light window, and backup plan. Solo is not an excuse for no plan.
