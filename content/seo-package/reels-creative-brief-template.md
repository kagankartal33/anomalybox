# Reels Creative Brief Template: Hook, Scene Flow, Proof and CTA

A **reels creative brief template** turns a communication problem into an approved creative direction. For Reels, it must also resolve first frame, vertical scene order, captions and completion.

## Quick answer

A usable **reels creative brief template** is a set of fields that force decisions: audience tension, opening frame, proof, spoken line, claim boundary and CTA. Copy the structure, then fill it with your product truth — empty templates do not reduce revisions by themselves.

## Write the single-message test

In **Reels Creative Brief Template**, complete this sentence: “After seeing this, the audience should understand or decide ___ because they saw ___.” If two different answers appear, split the concept.

## Translate tone into observable choices

In **Reels Creative Brief Template**, instead of “premium”, specify restrained pacing, material close-ups, controlled light and limited on-screen text. Instead of “authentic”, allow natural vocabulary, real context and an honest limitation.

## Platform execution

For Reels, record first frame, vertical scene order, captions and completion. The platform is not the strategy, but it changes how the idea opens, how long proof remains visible and what next action is natural.

## Example direction

```text
Project: reels creative brief template
Objective: help a qualified viewer choose between two approaches
Audience: a creator with limited weekly production time
Message: use the route that protects proof, not the route with the most scenes
Angle: a side-by-side planning decision
Opening: two briefs beside one hour on a timer
Proof: show the three-scene version completed within the constraint
CTA: save the decision rule
```

## Approval questions

- Does the angle answer the objective?
- Can the evidence be captured?
- Are facts, claims and disclosure resolved?
- Is creative freedom clear?
- Is there a simpler alternate route?

## How to fill the template without freezing

1. Write the viewer outcome in one sentence.  
2. List the proof you can show today (not ideal future proof).  
3. Script the opening 3 seconds as action + text, not a long monologue.  
4. Mark claim boundaries before you film.  
5. Record one backup ending (save CTA vs soft DM CTA).

## Common mistakes this template prevents

- Filming before the payoff is defined.  
- Using three hooks that all need different first frames.  
- Caption promising something the video never shows.  
- Leaving revision scope undefined for brand work.

## Reels brief (hook + 9:16 safe zones)

- 3 hook options

- Sound choice notes

Different from general social briefs: vertical pacing dominates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who writes the creative brief?

The strategist, creative lead, creator or brand may draft it. The people responsible for facts and execution should review it.

### How long should it be?

Usually one or two concise pages. References and appendices can sit separately.

### Can the creator change the hook?

Yes when the hook remains within the approved message and boundaries. Record which elements are flexible.

## Reels brief non-negotiables

- 3 hook options tested against mute  
- On-screen text ≤6 words  
- Beat timestamps  
- Safe-zone notes for UI chrome  
- CTA in final 3s  

General Instagram creative briefs omit vertical pacing detail — this template does not.

### Why three hooks?
Hooks fail quietly. Pre-writing three options prevents reshoots when the first second underperforms.

### Is this brief for ads or organic?
Both vertical formats. Paid cutdowns add product-proof requirements from the UGC list.
