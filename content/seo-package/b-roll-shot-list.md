# B-Roll Shot List for Creators: Proof, Texture and Transitions

A **b roll shot list** should describe why each shot exists, not merely list camera angles. The sequence below can be adapted to a creator who needs useful ideas without adding unnecessary production work and reduced when time or access is limited.

## Quick answer

In **b roll shot list**, connect the audience question to one opening frame and one visible form of proof. Keep the scene count within the time, access, and equipment available.

## Copyable shot list

| Shot | Purpose | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Hook frame | The first visible moment that communicates the problem or result. | Must have / optional |
| Context wide | Enough environment to understand where and how the action happens. | Must have / optional |
| Action medium | The main process, demonstration or interaction. | Must have / optional |
| Proof close-up | A detail that supports the point: a visible example, a specific process step, or a clear before-and-after decision. | Must have / optional |
| Result or comparison | A clear outcome, replay or side-by-side. | Must have / optional |
| CTA frame | A clean ending with the relevant next action. | Must have / optional |
| Backup proof | A simpler shot that can replace the main evidence if access changes. | Must have / optional |

## Build the list in message order

In **Context and action**, first decide what the viewer needs to understand. Then place the evidence in a logical sequence. You may film by location or setup for efficiency, but the planning document should still show the final narrative order.

### Hook frame

The opening image should communicate the same promise as the first line. Avoid an unrelated aesthetic shot followed by a completely different claim.

### Context and action

For **Ending**, use one wide or medium view to orient the viewer, then move closer for the meaningful action. Decorative B-roll should not replace the process the audience came to see.

### Proof

For this topic, useful proof can include a visible example, a specific process step, or a clear before-and-after decision. Label conditions when they affect the comparison, and do not design the concept around a claim that cannot be shown or sourced.

### Ending

In **Context and action**, end after the payoff. A CTA should be relevant: save the sequence, inspect the guide, compare current details, or take the next appropriate step. Avoid stacking several unrelated requests.

## A three-shot minimum version

1. **Hook:** show the problem or outcome.
2. **Proof:** demonstrate the decision or change.
3. **Next step:** give the viewer one action.

In **Context and action**, this version is valuable when the creator has limited time, confidence or access. It protects the message without pretending a simple setup is a full production.

## Quality checks before wrap

- Focus and exposure are usable on the proof shot.
- Required labels, disclosure and product details are visible.
- Continuity is consistent enough for the intended edit.
- The main action has at least one clean take.
- Audio has been checked, not assumed.
- Files are backed up before the set is cleared.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many shots should be on the list?

Only as many as the message requires. Six must-have shots and several optional alternatives are often more useful than dozens of unlabeled clips.

### Do I need every camera angle?

No. Choose angles that explain the action and evidence. Variety is secondary to clarity.

### Should B-roll be planned?

Yes, but after the main proof. Give B-roll a purpose such as transition, scale, atmosphere or continuity.

## Atmosphere B-roll bridge map

B-roll is **glue**, not the product story:

1. Establishing room / street (orientation)  
2. Insert detail that matches VO noun  
3. Movement bridge (walk, pour, open)  
4. Reaction cutaway when talking-head is long  
5. Texture loop for caption-safe holds  

**B-roll rule:** if the shot cannot cover a spoken phrase, drop it. UGC product demos are a different list entirely.

### Can B-roll replace a product demo?
No. B-roll covers VO and transitions; product mechanism still needs dedicated shots.

### How long should each B-roll clip be?
Capture 3–6 seconds of usable motion; edit shorter. Loops help caption-safe holds.
