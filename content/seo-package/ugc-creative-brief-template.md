# UGC Creative Brief Template for First-Round Clarity

A **ugc creative brief template** should protect the creator’s natural voice while making the brand’s objective, evidence, legal boundaries and commercial terms unambiguous.

## Quick answer

A **UGC creative brief template** locks product truth, hook variants, proof shots, usage rights and revision caps before the creator films. Missing rights terms create expensive disputes later.

## Copyable brief

```text
Audience problem:
Product role:
Concept angle:
Hook options:
Required demonstration:
Approved claims:
Prohibited claims:
Deliverables:
Usage rights:
Revision limit:
```

## Separate three kinds of requirements

1. **Legal and factual:** disclosure, approved claims, prohibited claims and permissions.
2. **Deliverable:** formats, lengths, dates, raw footage, variants and technical specifications.
3. **Creative:** audience tension, angle, tone, demonstration and room for creator adaptation.

Do not label every preference “mandatory”. That makes it difficult to identify the requirements that genuinely protect the campaign.

## Concept example

```text
Audience problem: the current routine feels complicated
Product role: replace one inconvenient step, not the entire routine
Angle: demonstrate where the product fits during a real morning
Hooks: “This is the step I kept skipping” / “A shorter routine that still feels complete”
Required proof: show the product in use and the resulting practical difference
Approved claims: insert exact approved facts and source
Prohibited claims: no guaranteed result, medical language or unsupported comparison
Deliverables: define aspect ratio, duration, variants and raw-footage terms
Usage: organic/paid term, territory, whitelisting and exclusivity
Feedback: one consolidated round through the named owner
```

## Revision control

State what counts as a correction—missing required fact, technical fault or departure from the approved concept—and what counts as a new concept. New direction should require a new agreement or fee.

## Rights and disclosure

Record organic and paid usage separately. Include term, territory, platforms, whitelisting, editing rights and exclusivity. Follow applicable advertising disclosure rules and platform requirements; the brief is not a substitute for legal advice.

## Approval checklist

- The creator understands the audience and single message.
- Mandatory claims are exact and sourced.
- The product demonstration is possible and honest.
- The creator knows what may be adapted.
- Rights, payment and revisions are documented elsewhere in the contract.
- One person consolidates feedback.

## How to fill the template without freezing

1. Write the viewer outcome in one sentence.  
2. List the proof you can show today (not ideal future proof).  
3. Script the opening 3 seconds as action + text, not a long monologue.  
4. Mark claim boundaries before you film.  
5. Record one backup ending (save CTA vs soft DM CTA).

## Common mistakes this template prevents

- Filming before the payoff is defined.  
- Using three hooks that all need different first frames.  
- Caption promising something the video never shows.  
- Leaving revision scope undefined for brand work.

## UGC brief essentials

- Product truth + claims boundary  
- 2–3 hook variants for ad testing  
- Required proof shots (macro, demo, reaction)  
- Usage window (organic / paid / spark)  
- Revision rounds included  
- Raw file requirements  

**UGC-specific risk:** unpaid perpetual usage. Put duration and media in the brief, not the DMs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a brand provide a full script?

Only when exact wording is required. Otherwise, beats and approved facts usually preserve a more natural creator voice.

### Can the brand request unlimited revisions?

That should not be assumed. Define revision scope and new-concept work in the agreement.

### Is the creative brief a contract?

No. Use a contract for payment, ownership, rights, cancellation and other legal terms.
