# Instagram Reels Shot List: Build the Video Before You Film

A **instagram reels shot list** should describe why each shot exists, not merely list camera angles. The sequence below can be adapted to an Instagram creator choosing what to publish and how to make it fit the account and reduced when time or access is limited.

## Quick answer

The practical version of **instagram reels shot list** starts with a specific audience need, an opening image, credible evidence, and a production boundary. Resolve those choices before adding scenes.

## Copyable shot list

| Shot | Purpose | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Hook frame | The first visible moment that communicates the problem or result. | Must have / optional |
| Context wide | Enough environment to understand where and how the action happens. | Must have / optional |
| Action medium | The main process, demonstration or interaction. | Must have / optional |
| Proof close-up | A detail that supports the point: a screen-visible example, audience response pattern, product demonstration or process detail. | Must have / optional |
| Result or comparison | A clear outcome, replay or side-by-side. | Must have / optional |
| CTA frame | A clean ending with the relevant next action. | Must have / optional |
| Backup proof | A simpler shot that can replace the main evidence if access changes. | Must have / optional |

## Build the list in message order

In **Ending**, first decide what the viewer needs to understand. Then place the evidence in a logical sequence. You may film by location or setup for efficiency, but the planning document should still show the final narrative order.

### Hook frame

The opening image should communicate the same promise as the first line. Avoid an unrelated aesthetic shot followed by a completely different claim.

### Context and action

For **Context and action**, use one wide or medium view to orient the viewer, then move closer for the meaningful action. Decorative B-roll should not replace the process the audience came to see.

### Proof

For this topic, useful proof can include a screen-visible example, audience response pattern, product demonstration or process detail. Label conditions when they affect the comparison, and do not design the concept around a claim that cannot be shown or sourced.

### Ending

In **Hook frame**, end after the payoff. A CTA should be relevant: save the sequence, inspect the guide, compare current details, or take the next appropriate step. Avoid stacking several unrelated requests.

## A three-shot minimum version

1. **Hook:** show the problem or outcome.
2. **Proof:** demonstrate the decision or change.
3. **Next step:** give the viewer one action.

In **Ending**, this version is valuable when the creator has limited time, confidence or access. It protects the message without pretending a simple setup is a full production.

## Quality checks before wrap

- Focus and exposure are usable on the proof shot.
- Required labels, disclosure and product details are visible.
- Continuity is consistent enough for the intended edit.
- The main action has at least one clean take.
- Audio has been checked, not assumed.
- Files are backed up before the set is cleared.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many shots should be on the list?

Only as many as the message requires. Six must-have shots and several optional alternatives are often more useful than dozens of unlabeled clips.

### Do I need every camera angle?

No. Choose angles that explain the action and evidence. Variety is secondary to clarity.

### Should B-roll be planned?

Yes, but after the main proof. Give B-roll a purpose such as transition, scale, atmosphere or continuity.
