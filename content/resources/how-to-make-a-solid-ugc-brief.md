## What the handoff must settle

Revision rounds multiply when the brief leaves product truth, usage and proof open to interpretation. Here, the issue starts when **product truth & claims** is planned without **visual proof requirements**. The draft may look good. Yet the creator still does not know what to film or approve.

Start with the smallest version of **product truth & claims** that delivers fewer avoidable revisions. Check it against a brief-to-final-frame check. Keep this boundary in mind: Do not improvise a product claim on set. If proof is not ready, tighten the promise. Do not mask it with buzzwords.

## Frequently asked questions

### What must Product truth & claims decide first?

Set one clear default for **product truth & claims** next to **visual proof requirements**. State the exact condition that justifies a change. A brief-to-final-frame check is far more useful than a long reference deck because you can test it directly on set.

### How can Visual proof requirements be tested with simple gear?

Protect the proof moment above all else. Cut optional shots first. A short version works well when **visual proof requirements** stays visible and claims stay inside your approved boundary.

### When should Edit direction be updated?

Update **edit direction** when repeated passes on **script & scene breakdown** show the same friction. A shift in audience, offer, or weekly capacity also justifies an update. A single slow video is not a reason to rebuild your whole system.

### How long should this workflow take for How to Make a Solid UGC Brief?

Aim for 45 minutes total production for **How to Make a Solid UGC Brief**. Spend 15 minutes setting up your mic and camera, film 3 takes, and confirm a brief-to-final-frame check before tearing down.
